Important Note!

Fold Out Flaps: Your study of this Operating Manual will be greatly facilitated if you fold out the flaps at the rear of this booklet (Pages 53–56).

By folding out the rear diagrams, you will have immediate and continuous access to the large perspective illustrations of the machine (Fig. 1 — Front View and Fig. 2 — Rear View) with numeral references (1 to 40) indicating all controls, thread guides, and mechanisms.

Needle System 705 (15x1)

Your Husqvarna Automatic utilizes standard flat-shank sewing machine needles — System 705 (also known as 15x1). Never use needles designed for other industrial or rotary machines.

  • Always ensure the needle is inserted with the flat side facing away from you (towards the back).
  • The long thread groove running down the blade must face directly towards you.
  • Push the needle up into the needle clamp as far as it will go before tightening the clamp screw securely.

Machine Identification

Each Husqvarna Class 21 machine is stamped with a serial number located on the underside of the base plate and on the rating plate at the rear. When ordering genuine replacement parts or accessories, always state the exact model designation and serial number.

100% Jam-Proof Shuttle: Your Husqvarna machine is equipped with an exclusive patented drop-in non-jamming rotary hook. Even if thread should tangle, the shuttle will not seize or jam the gears.

Electric Specifications

Check that the voltage indicated on the machine's motor specification plate matches your electrical mains supply before connecting the foot control.

Husqvarna Automatic Class 21Setup • Inserting Needle & Electrical Connection

How to Insert a Needle

Turn the handwheel towards you until the needle bar (7) is at its highest point.

  1. Loosen the needle clamp screw (A, Fig. 3).
  2. Insert the needle into the needle clamp (B), pushing it upwards as far as it will go.
  3. Ensure the flat side of the needle shank faces away from you (to the rear) and the long thread groove faces directly towards you.
  4. Tighten the needle screw securely with the screwdriver.

Removing the Bobbin Case from the Shuttle

Turn the hand wheel (19, Fig. 1) towards you until the needle is in its highest position. Open the shuttle access door (26). Take hold of the bobbin case with the thumb and forefinger (Fig. 4), so that the latch (A, Fig. 5) is depressed, and lift the case out. As long as the latch is held open, the bobbin cannot fall out.

Husqvarna Automatic Class 21Bobbin Winding

Bobbin Winding is Simple

Winding bobbins on the Husqvarna Automatic is fast and automatic:

  1. Disengage the sewing mechanism by turning the large stop motion knob inside the handwheel counter-clockwise.
  2. Place a spool of thread on the spool pin (41).
  3. Lead the thread through thread guide (40), down between the bobbin tension discs (35), and across to the bobbin winder spindle (33) as shown in Fig. 6.
  4. Place an empty bobbin on spindle (33) with the small pin entering the hole in the bobbin side.
  5. Push the bobbin winder frame towards the handwheel until it clicks into engagement (Fig. 5).
  6. Run the motor. The winder disengages automatically when full.

Husqvarna Automatic Class 21Bobbin Case • Lower Threading

Threading the Bobbin Case

To remove the bobbin case, open the shuttle cover under the free arm. Pull open the bobbin case latch (A, Fig. 7) and draw the case straight out.

Inserting Bobbin into Case

  1. Hold the bobbin case in your left hand with the open cylinder facing you.
  2. Hold the wound bobbin in your right hand so the thread leads off from the top from left to right (clockwise).
  3. Insert the bobbin into the case.
  4. Draw the thread into slot (A, Fig. 8) on the edge of the case.
  5. Pull the thread under the tension spring until it enters the delivery eyelet (B, Fig. 8). Leave about 4 inches of thread trailing.

Husqvarna Automatic Class 21Upper Threading • Bobbin Insertion

Inserting Case & Upper Threading

Inserting Bobbin Case: Turn handwheel until needle is raised. Hold case by the open latch, align the case finger with the notch at the top of the race, and press into place until it snaps securely shut.

Threading the Upper Thread

  1. Turn handwheel to raise take-up lever (13) to its highest position. Raise the presser foot lifter.
  2. From spool pin (41), pass thread through guide (14).
  3. Lead thread down and between tension discs (10).
  4. Loop around take-up spring (12) and slash guide (9).
  5. Thread through the eyelet of take-up lever (13) from right to left (Fig. 10).
  6. Down through lower guides (8 & 4), and through the needle eye from front to back.

Husqvarna Automatic Class 21Tension Adjustment • Stitch Balance

Correct Thread Tension

A perfect lockstitch locks both threads evenly in the center of the fabric thickness without puckering or loops:

  • Correct Tension (Fig. 12, top): Upper and lower threads lock smoothly between the fabric layers.
  • Upper Tension Too Tight (Fig. 12, center): Upper thread lies straight along top of fabric; lower thread is pulled up. Lower the numbered dial setting.
  • Upper Tension Too Loose (Fig. 12, bottom): Lower thread lies straight; upper loops appear on underside. Increase dial setting.

The graduated tension regulator knob (Fig. 11) is calibrated with numbers. For normal straight stitching on medium fabric, the standard setting is between 3 and 5.

Husqvarna Automatic Class 21Straight Stitch • Stitch Length • Reverse

Now We Are Ready to Sew

Drawing up Lower Thread: Hold the upper needle thread loosely with your left hand. Turn handwheel toward you one full turn until the needle descends and rises again. Pull upper thread gently to bring up a loop of lower thread. Place both threads under the presser foot and towards the rear.

Stitch Length & Reverse Feed

The stitch length knob (20, Fig. 14) regulates stitch length from 0 to 4 mm. Turn the knob clockwise for shorter stitches, counter-clockwise for longer stitches.

Reverse Stitching: To secure the ends of a seam without turning the fabric, simply press down the reverse feed knob (21). The machine sews in reverse as long as the lever is depressed, and instantly resumes forward sewing when released.

Speed Reducer for Extra Slow Sewing

Your Husqvarna Automatic is equipped with an exclusive built-in 5:1 mechanical reduction gear inside the handwheel. This revolutionary feature allows you to sew stitch-by-stitch at exceptionally slow speeds with five times the penetrating power!

How to Engage the Gear:

  1. Grasp the handwheel firmly with your left hand.
  2. Pull out the reduction gear control knob (34, Fig. 15) and turn it slightly until it locks in the outer position (Fig. 16).
  3. The machine now operates in low gear — ideal for thick seams, heavy denim, leather, delicate applique, and precision buttonholes.

To return to high speed, press the knob back in while rocking the handwheel slightly.

Husqvarna Automatic Class 21Gathering • Gathering Foot

Gathering Techniques

Gathering can be done in two ways on the Husqvarna Automatic:

1. Gathering with Gathering Foot (Fig. 20)

Attach the special gathering foot (Part 3019). Set stitch width to 0, stitch length to maximum (4 mm), and tighten upper thread tension slightly. Place fabric under foot; the machine gathers the fabric evenly as you sew.

2. Gathering Over a Cord

Set machine for narrow zigzag (width 2). Lay a strong cord or perle cotton thread on top of the fabric and sew over it with zigzag stitches, taking care not to pierce the cord with the needle. Then pull the cord to gather the fabric to the exact desired fullness (Fig. 21).

Husqvarna Automatic Class 21Narrow Hems • Hemmer Foot

Making Narrow Hems

The narrow hemmer foot (Part 40 3035, Fig. 22) turns and stitches a neat, double-folded narrow hem in one single operation — ideal for handkerchiefs, ruffles, and shirts.

How to Use the Hemmer Foot:

  1. Fold edge of fabric twice for about 1/8 inch and insert into the scroll of the hemmer foot.
  2. Lower presser foot and sew two or three stitches.
  3. Gently hold the needle thread tails behind the foot while guiding the raw edge steadily into the mouth of the scroll (Fig. 23).
  4. Do not push or stretch fabric; let the feed dog pull the fabric naturally.

Can be sewn with straight stitch or decorative zigzag stitches.

Three Needle Positions (L, C, R)

The needle position control lever (16, Fig. 25) shifts the needle’s line of travel to three distinct positions without moving the fabric:

Husqvarna Automatic Class 21Buttonhole Sewing

Using Zigzag to Sew Buttonholes

Attach buttonhole foot (41 15428, Fig. 26). Set stitch length between 0.2 and 0.5 (satin stitch), needle position L.

Step-by-Step Buttonhole Sequence (Fig. 26a):

  1. Left Bead: Set stitch width to 2. Sew the left side to the desired buttonhole length. Stop with needle raised.
  2. First Bar Tack: Set width to 4, needle position M/C. Take 4 to 5 stitches to form the bottom bar tack.
  3. Right Bead: Set width to 2, needle position R. Sew back along the right side in reverse.
  4. Second Bar Tack: Set width to 4, needle position M/C. Take 4 to 5 stitches for the top bar tack.
  5. Fasten Threads: Set width to 0, take 2 to 3 securing stitches. Cut buttonhole open with knife (41 15399).

Husqvarna Automatic Class 21Sewing on Buttons & Hooks

Sewing on Buttons and Fasteners

Attach button sewing foot (41 15237). Lower the feed dog by turning the drop feed knob (25) clockwise.

Sewing Flat Buttons (Fig. 27):

  1. Place button on marked position under the foot so holes line up with the foot opening.
  2. Set needle position to L and lower presser foot.
  3. Turn handwheel by hand to check that needle enters left hole cleanly.
  4. Adjust stitch width control (24) until needle enters right hole precisely without hitting button.
  5. Run machine for 6 to 8 stitches. Set width to 0 and take 2 securing stitches.

Thread Shank (Fig. 28): For heavy coats, lay a needle or pin over the button between the holes before stitching to create a thread shank. Also ideal for hooks and eyes (Fig. 29).

Hemstitching & Weaver's Reed

Traditional openwork hemstitching can be sewn effortlessly using the special hemstitcher attachment (Part 41 15367) or with the weaver's reed (Part 40 9001).

Technique (Fig. 30 & 31):

Rolled Edges and Overcasting

To produce dainty rolled edges on sheer fabrics, evening gowns, and scarves, use the narrow hemmer foot with zigzag stitching (Fig. 32).

Husqvarna Automatic Class 21Blindstitching • Invisible Hems

Blindstitching of Hems

Perhaps you have just finished a dress, skirt, or pair of curtains and want an invisible hem without tedious hand sewing. The Husqvarna Automatic sews authentic blind hems quickly and easily!

Folding & Setting (Fig. 34–36):

  1. Fold and press the hem to the desired width.
  2. Fold the hem back under the garment body, leaving about 1/4 inch (6 mm) of the hem allowance projecting (Fig. 35).
  3. Set machine to automatic blindstitch pattern (Cam A or B, Pattern 4).
  4. Adjust stitch width so the wide zigzag stitch catches only a single fiber of the folded garment edge. On the right side, the stitches are completely invisible!

Husqvarna Automatic Class 21Zipper Insertion • Zipper Foot

It's Easier to Sew on a Zipper Than You Think!

Attach adjustable zipper foot (41 15236, Fig. 52). This specialized foot allows stitching immediately alongside the raised zipper teeth without slipping:

  1. Baste zipper opening closed. Press seam open.
  2. Loosen adjustment screw on zipper foot and slide foot to the right of the needle to sew the left zipper tape.
  3. Slide foot to the left of the needle to sew the right zipper tape.

  5. Remove basting stitches. The zipper opens smoothly and lies completely flat and concealed.

Darning worn spots and tears with straight stitching is fast and neat:

Regular cleaning ensures silent running and decades of peak performance. The 100% jam-proof shuttle requires only occasional cleaning of lint and dust.

Look After Your Husqvarna Automatic

A sewing machine needs proper care

It is surprising how many people have never taken the trouble to find out how a sewing machine should be cared for. True, sewing machines are unusually tough pieces of equipment, which will go on working for years without being oiled or cleaned. But they do not like it, and they proclaim their dislike by running roughly and making a whining, scraping noise.

Like any precision-made machine, the Husqvarna Automatic will always operate smoothly, silently and efficiently if it is properly attended to and oiled regularly.
